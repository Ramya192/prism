== 1005.2(m) — Definition of "Unauthorized Electronic Fund Transfer" ==
An unauthorized EFT is "an electronic fund transfer from a consumer's
account initiated by a person other than the consumer without actual
authority" and that provides no benefit to the consumer. Three explicit
exclusions matter for fraud review:
1. A person the consumer themself "furnished the access device to"
   remains an AUTHORIZED user even if they exceed whatever limit the
   consumer meant to grant them — unless and until the consumer notifies
   the institution to revoke that authority. In practice: a family
   member or employee given a debit card who then overspends is not
   committing an "unauthorized" transfer under this definition, however
   much the consumer disputes it after the fact.
2. A transfer made "with fraudulent intent by the consumer or any person
   acting in concert with the consumer" is explicitly NOT an unauthorized
   transfer — a consumer cannot claim their own staged/collusive fraud as
   an unauthorized-transfer dispute.
3. A transfer "by the financial institution or its employee" is not an
   unauthorized transfer under this definition (it may be a different
   kind of error — see 1005.11 below).

== 1005.6 — Consumer Liability for Unauthorized Transfers ==
Liability scales with how quickly the consumer reports the loss/theft of
an access device or the unauthorized transfer, in three tiers:
- Tier 1 (prompt notice): "If the consumer notifies the financial
  institution within two business days after learning of the loss or
  theft of the access device, the consumer's liability shall not exceed
  the lesser of $50 or the amount of unauthorized transfers that occur
  before notice to the financial institution."
- Tier 2 (delayed notice, beyond 2 business days but reported): liability
  up to $500 — up to $50 for the first two business days, plus any
  additional unauthorized transfers before notice, PROVIDED the
  institution can show that timely reporting would have prevented those
  additional transfers.
- Tier 3 (statement-period notice): "A consumer must report an
  unauthorized electronic fund transfer that appears on a periodic
  statement within 60 days of the financial institution's transmittal of
  the statement to avoid liability for subsequent transfers" — beyond 60
  days, liability is effectively unlimited for transfers occurring after
  that point that further notice would have prevented.
These liability limits only apply once the institution has met its own
disclosure obligations and provided an access device with an acceptable
means of identifying the consumer; reasonable extensions apply for
extenuating circumstances (e.g., extended travel, hospitalization).

== 1005.11 — Procedures for Resolving Errors ==
"Error," for dispute-handling purposes, is a defined term broader than
just unauthorized transfers — 1005.11(a)(1) lists: an unauthorized EFT;
an incorrect EFT to or from the consumer's account; an EFT omitted from
a periodic statement; a computational or bookkeeping error by the
institution; an incorrect amount received from an electronic terminal;
a transfer not properly identified per 1005.9/1005.10(a); and a request
for documentation or clarification about a specific transfer. Routine
balance inquiries, tax-record requests, and duplicate documentation
requests are explicitly NOT "errors" under this definition.

Consumer notification timeline: the consumer must report the alleged
error within 60 days after the institution sent the periodic statement
reflecting it (or, for a documentation-based request, within 60 days of
receiving that documentation).

Institution investigation timeline:
- Standard: investigate within "10 business days of receiving a notice
  of error" and report results within 3 business days after concluding.
- Extended (45-day option): if investigation can't finish in 10 business
  days, the institution gets up to 45 calendar days total — but ONLY if
  it provisionally credits the consumer's account (including applicable
  interest) within the original 10-business-day window.
- New-account extension: for transfers within 30 days of the account's
  first deposit, the standard window extends to 20 business days (and
  the extended option to 90 calendar days).
- POS/foreign-transaction extension: the 45-day extended investigation
  period extends to 90 calendar days for point-of-sale debit card
  transactions and for transfers not initiated within a state.

A dispute resolved outside these timelines, or an "unauthorized
transaction" claim that actually falls under the 1005.2(m) authorized-
user or consumer-fraudulent-intent exclusions above, is a compliance-
relevant inconsistency worth surfacing during document/statement review
— distinct from, and in addition to, whatever a transaction's own
amount/pattern-based fraud score already says about it.
