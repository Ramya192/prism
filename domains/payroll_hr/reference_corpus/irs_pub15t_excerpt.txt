== Introduction — Purpose and Scope ==
"This publication supplements Pub. 15, Employer's Tax Guide. It describes
how to figure withholding using the Wage Bracket Method or Percentage
Method." Two methods exist for computing federal income tax withholding
from wages: the Percentage Method (Worksheet 1A, works for any wage
amount and any Form W-4 year) and the Wage Bracket Method (Worksheet 2,
a lookup-table approach that "cover[s] a limited amount of annual wages
(generally, less than $100,000)" — above that, the employer must use the
Percentage Method instead). A payslip whose stated withholding cannot be
reconciled to either method, for the employee's stated pay period and
filing status, is a documentation gap worth flagging even before asking
whether the withholding amount itself looks numerically right.

== What's New for 2026 ==
The 2026 withholding tables were updated for P.L. 119-21 ("One Big
Beautiful Bill Act"), including "the permanent extension of individual
tax rates, the permanent extension of the increased standard deduction,
and the permanent termination of personal exemptions" (originally enacted
by the 2017 Tax Cuts and Jobs Act). The 2026 Form W-4 added a new
checkbox below Step 4(c) for claiming exemption from withholding — an
employee marked "Exempt" should show $0 federal withholding regardless of
gross pay, which is a legitimate zero, not a missing-field error.

== Form W-4 — How Its Steps Drive Withholding ==
Form W-4 has 5 steps. Every employer-held Form W-4 should show a
completed Step 1 (name, address, SSN, filing status) and a signed Step 5.
Steps 2, 3, and 4 are optional and each has a specific, distinct effect
on the withholding calculation:
- "Step 2. If the employee checks the box in Step 2, the employer figures
  withholding from the 'Form W-4, Step 2, Checkbox' column ... This
  results in higher withholding for the employee."
- "Step 3. Employers use the amount on this line as an annual reduction
  in the amount of withholding" (dependent/credit amount).
- "Steps 4(a) and 4(b). Employers increase the annual amount of wages
  subject to income tax withholding by the annual amount shown in Step
  4(a) and reduce [it] by the annual amount shown in Step 4(b)" (other
  income, and deductions, respectively).
- "Step 4(c). Employers will increase withholding by the per pay period
  tax amount in Step 4(c)" (a flat extra amount per paycheck).
A new employee who submits no Form W-4 at all "will be treated as if
they had checked the box for Single or Married filing separately in Step
1(c) and made no entries in Step 2, Step 3, or Step 4" — i.e., maximum
default withholding, not zero withholding.

== Rounding ==
"To figure the income tax to withhold, you may reduce the last digit of
the wages to zero, or figure the wages to the nearest dollar. You may
also round the tax for the pay period to the nearest dollar. If rounding
is used, it must be used consistently. Withheld tax amounts should be
rounded to the nearest whole dollar by dropping amounts under 50 cents
and increasing amounts from 50 to 99 cents to the next dollar. For
example, $2.30 becomes $2 and $2.50 becomes $3." A payslip whose
withholding figures don't round to whole dollars consistently within the
same document is a low-severity documentation inconsistency, not
necessarily fraud on its own.

Amount," tentative annual withholding at the bracket floor, and marginal

  $19,300–$44,100: $0.00 + 10% of the amount over $19,300
  $44,100–$120,100: $2,480.00 + 12% of the amount over $44,100
  $120,100–$230,700: $11,600.00 + 22% of the amount over $120,100

These are ANNUALIZED brackets — a per-paycheck withholding figure must
first be annualized (multiplied by the number of pay periods per year:
52 weekly, 26 biweekly, 24 semimonthly, 12 monthly) before comparing it
against this table.

== 2026 Wage Bracket Method — WEEKLY Payroll Period (representative rows) ==
"Standard withholding" columns, "Adjusted Wage Amount" (line 1h of
Worksheet 2) at least / but less than, tentative withholding amount —
real published dollar figures, a representative sample spanning the low
end of the table through a mid-range wage:
  Single/MFS, no Step 2 checkbox:
    $0–$315: $0   $625–$635: $1   $785–$795: $53   $1,205–$1,215: $103
    $1,435–$1,445: $147   $1,915–$1,925: $253
  Married Filing Jointly, no Step 2 checkbox:
    $0–$625: $0   $785–$795: $17   $1,205–$1,215: $61   $1,435–$1,445: $89
    $1,915–$1,925: $147
  Head of Household, no Step 2 checkbox:
    $0–$315: $0   $465–$475: $1   $785–$795: $33   $1,205–$1,215: $83
    $1,915–$1,925: $184
A weekly-paid employee whose Adjusted Wage Amount falls in the $785–$795
bracket, filing Single with no Step 2 checkbox, has a Tentative
Withholding Amount of exactly $53 before Step 3/4(c) adjustments — a
withholding figure far outside this looked-up value for the employee's
stated wage and filing status (absent a Step 3 credit or Step 4(c) extra
withholding entry on file) is inconsistent with the published tables.

The Biweekly table's brackets are not simply double the Weekly table's —
each pay-frequency table is independently published, so a payslip's
implied annual wage should be checked against the STANDARD Withholding
Rate Schedules (above) when comparing figures across different pay
frequencies, not against a doubled/halved Weekly or Biweekly bracket.
