== 10.1 — Claims That Are Incomplete or Contain Invalid Information ==
(Rev. 145, 04-23-04)
"If a claim is submitted with incomplete or invalid information, it may
be returned to the submitter as unprocessable." This is the manual's
core validity rule: a claim is not "denied" for incomplete data, it is
returned as unprocessable — a distinct administrative status from a
denial for lack of medical necessity or coverage.

== 10.2 — Required Patient and Insured Information ==
Item 1a (patient's Medicare Beneficiary Identifier) and Item 2 (patient's
name as shown on the Medicare card) are explicitly marked "required
field[s]." Item 11 is a required field with a specific compliance
implication: "BY COMPLETING THIS ITEM, THE PHYSICIAN/SUPPLIER
ACKNOWLEDGES HAVING MADE A GOOD FAITH EFFORT TO DETERMINE WHETHER
MEDICARE IS THE PRIMARY OR SECONDARY PAYER." If insurance primary to
Medicare exists, item 11 must contain the insured's policy/group number
and items 4, 6, and 7 must also be completed — an incomplete secondary-
payer determination is itself a compliance flag, not just a missing field.

Medicare Secondary Payer (MSP) circumstances listed explicitly: Group
Health Plan Coverage (Working Aged, Disability/Large Group Health Plan,
End Stage Renal Disease), No Fault and/or Other Liability, and
Work-Related Illness/Injury (Workers' Compensation, Black Lung, Veterans
Benefits). For a paper claim to be considered for MSP benefits, a copy
of the primary payer's Explanation of Benefits (EOB) notice must be
forwarded along with the claim form.

== 10.4 — Provider/Supplier Information: Referring/Ordering Physicians ==
The ordering/referring requirement (effective January 1, 1992, required
by §1833(q) of the Social Security Act) mandates that "[a]ll claims for
Medicare covered services and items that are the result of a physician's
order or referral shall include the ordering/referring physician's
name." Effective May 23, 2008, this must be the physician's National
Provider Identifier (NPI), not just a name — "all provider identifiers
submitted on the CMS-1500 claim form MUST be in the form of an NPI."
Categories of services explicitly requiring referring/ordering
information: parenteral and enteral nutrition, immunosuppressive drug
claims, Hepatitis B claims, diagnostic laboratory services, diagnostic
radiology services, portable x-ray services, consultative services,
durable medical equipment, and (effective for dates of service on or
after October 1, 2012) physical therapy, occupational therapy, and
speech-language pathology services.

== 10.5 / 24B — Place of Service (POS) and Setting Consistency ==
Item 24B (place of service code) is a required field identifying "the
setting" for each service line. The manual gives a concrete consistency
check: "When a service is rendered to a patient who is a registered
inpatient or an outpatient... of a hospital, use the inpatient hospital
POS code 21, Off Campus-Outpatient Hospital POS code 19, or On
Campus-Outpatient Hospital POS code 22, respectively." A claim whose
billed procedure type is inconsistent with its stated place of service
(e.g., an inpatient-only procedure billed under an outpatient POS code)
is a documented mismatch pattern this section exists to prevent.

== Item 21 — Diagnosis Coding Rules ==
Up to 12 diagnosis codes may be entered (form version 02/12), each
"[using] the highest level of specificity," entered in priority order.
Two explicit validity rules: "Do not report both ICD-9-CM and ICD-10-CM
codes on the same claim form" (a claim spanning both eras must be split
into separate claims by date), and "Do not insert a period in the
ICD-9-CM or ICD-10-CM code." Item 24E requires each service line to
reference a specific diagnosis code letter from item 21 — a service
line with no valid diagnosis reference, or one referencing a diagnosis
code that doesn't exist in item 21, is an internally inconsistent claim.

== Item 24D — Procedure Coding and Unlisted/NOC Codes ==
Procedures are billed via HCPCS codes. For "unlisted procedure code[s]"
or "Not Otherwise Classified" (NOC) codes, the manual sets an explicit
completeness rule: a narrative description is required either in item
19 or as an attachment. "Return as unprocessable if an 'unlisted
procedure code' or a NOC code is indicated in item 24d, but an
accompanying narrative is not present in item 19 or on an attachment."
NOC laboratory tests specifically must include "the specific name of the
laboratory test(s) and/or a short descriptor" or be returned
unprocessable per Pub. 100-04, Chapter 1, Section 80.3.2.

== Item 24A — Service Dates ==
"Return as unprocessable if a date of service extends more than 1 day,
and a valid 'to' date is not present" — an open-ended date range without
a closing date is treated as an incomplete claim, not silently accepted.

== Physician Definition (§1861(r) of the Social Security Act) ==
The manual defines "physician," for claim-completion purposes, as
limited to five specific practitioner types: (1) a doctor of medicine or
osteopathy; (2) a doctor of dental surgery or dental medicine; (3) a
doctor of podiatric medicine (for specified statutory purposes only);
(4) a doctor of optometry (for specified statutory purposes only); or
(5) a licensed chiropractor, and only with respect to manual spinal
manipulation to correct a subluxation. A claim listing a referring or
performing "physician" outside these five categories, without the
non-physician-practitioner rules of Pub. 100-02 Chapter 15 being
invoked, does not meet this manual's definition.
