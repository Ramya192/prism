== 1.3 — Convertible Virtual Currency (CVC), Defined ==
"'Virtual currency' refers to a medium of exchange that can operate like
currency but does not have all the attributes of 'real' currency ...
including legal tender status. CVC is a type of virtual currency that
either has an equivalent value as currency, or acts as a substitute for
currency." The label used for a given CVC ("digital currency,"
"cryptocurrency," "cryptoasset," "digital asset," etc.) is explicitly
"not dispositive of its regulatory treatment" — FinCEN looks at the
underlying activity and facts/circumstances, not the marketing label,
and applies regardless of whether the ledger is centralized or
distributed or what transmission technology is used.

== 1.2 / 2 — Money Transmitter and Money Transmission, Defined ==
A "money transmitter" is a "person that provides money transmission
services," or "any other person engaged in the transfer of funds."
"Money transmission services" means "the acceptance of currency, funds,
or other value that substitutes for currency from one person and the
transmission of currency, funds, or other value that substitutes for
currency to another location or person by any means." A person qualifies
as a money transmitter regardless of: order of operations (accepting
value first vs. transmitting first), whether operating transactionally
or on an ongoing account basis, and regardless of "the type of asset the
person uses as value that substitutes for currency, or whether such
asset is physical or virtual."

Six activities are explicitly EXCLUDED from "money transmitter" status
(31 CFR § 1010.100(ff)(5)(ii)(A)-(F)): (a) providing only the
delivery/communication/network access used by an actual money
transmitter; (b) acting as a payment processor for a good/service sale
through a clearance/settlement system by agreement with the seller;
(c) operating a clearance/settlement system solely between BSA-regulated
institutions; (d) physically transporting currency/value with no more
than custodial interest (e.g., an armored car); (e) providing prepaid
access; or (f) accepting/transmitting funds only integral to a sale of
goods or services other than money transmission itself. "FinCEN
interprets these exemptions strictly" — a business does not get an
exemption for activity that only partially conforms to one.

== 2.1 — AML Program Requirements for Money Services Businesses (MSBs) ==
A non-exempt money transmitter must register with FinCEN as an MSB
"within 180 days of starting to engage in money transmission," and must
maintain a written AML program that, at minimum: "(a) incorporate[s]
policies, procedures and internal controls reasonably designed to assure
ongoing compliance (including verifying customer identification, filing
reports, creating and retaining records, and responding to law
enforcement requests); (b) designate[s] an individual responsible to
assure day-to-day compliance; (c) provide[s] training for appropriate
personnel, including training in the detection of suspicious
transactions; and, (d) provide[s] for independent review to monitor and
maintain an adequate program." The program must be risk-based, scaled to
"the composition of customer base, the geographies served, and the
financial products and services offered."

== 4.1 — Peer-to-Peer (P2P) Exchangers ==
"P2P exchangers are (typically) natural persons engaged in the business
of buying and selling CVCs," often advertising through classifieds,
dedicated platforms, forums, or word of mouth, and "facilitate transfers
from one type of CVC to a different type of CVC, as well as exchanges
between CVC and other types of value." A natural person acting as a P2P
exchanger "must comply with BSA regulations as a money transmitter
acting as principal ... regardless of the regularity or formality of
such transactions" — UNLESS the activity is infrequent and not for
profit or gain, in which case it is exempt. A counterparty on a wallet
statement that repeatedly appears in a pattern consistent with P2P-
exchanger activity (frequent small counterparty-initiated CVC-for-CVC or
CVC-for-fiat conversions) is a business-model fact worth surfacing
during document review, distinct from any single transaction's own
fraud score.

== 4.2 / 4.2.1 — CVC Wallets: Hosted vs. Unhosted ==
"CVC wallets are interfaces for storing and transferring CVCs" —
technology varies (mobile, software, hardware), and wallets may store
value locally or hold a private key controlling value stored elsewhere.
The regulatory distinction that matters is control, not technology:
"Wallets where user funds are controlled by third parties are called
'hosted wallets' whereas wallets where users control the funds are
called 'unhosted wallets.'" Whether a wallet-hosting intermediary itself
has BSA obligations depends on four facts: "(a) who owns the value; (b)
where the value is stored; (c) whether the owner interacts directly with
the payment system where the CVC runs; and, (d) whether the person
acting as intermediary has total independent control over the value."
Hosted wallet providers are, per FinCEN, "account-based money
transmitters that receive, store, and transmit CVCs" on the user's
behalf — a materially different compliance posture than an unhosted,
self-custodied wallet, which is relevant context when a wallet
statement's counterparty is itself a wallet-hosting platform rather than
another individual user.
